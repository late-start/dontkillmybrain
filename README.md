There's a growing body of research ([dontkillmybrain.com/research](https://dontkillmybrain.com/research)) about the harmful effects of AI usage. A species that has already been lured by convenience into an era of shortening attention spans and unthinking acceptance of propaganda would do well to heed these warnings.

There's a reason our brains have been so easy to hack. It's because thinking is expensive on a metabolic level and we're evolutionarily hardwired to look for shortcuts. Propaganda machines aren't new, but hyper-targeting is, as is this ever more compelling substitute for thought. And like social media before them, chatbots are engineered to keep you engaged, not to keep you thinking.

I'm far from an AI doomer. I collaborate with it daily to get work done and I think there's actually plenty of good to be done with this tech. We're seeing examples of that daily, whether in healthcare, education, or sustainability. However, our regulatory machines are broken, out of touch, or both, and there isn't nearly enough being done to address the dangers.

We're already seeing "my LLM vs. yours" dynamics on X that take the shape of: blob is the only LLM that will agree with your pre-existing bias. I'm terrified by how many people are asking AI to make judgments on their behalf and accepting LLM output as authoritative expertise. These are still (very advanced) stochastic parrots trained to tell you what you want to hear. Constrain probability space sufficiently and you can get very usable output, but you should be the one intentionally doing the constraining, not (just) the makers of the chatbot.

How do you know whether you're still thinking? Run your chats through [dontkillmybrain.com](https://dontkillmybrain.com) to analyze how you're using your chatbot. It reads your conversations turn by turn and flags where you stopped thinking. It's not perfect, but it's a start and should at least stimulate some metacognition even if it gets the classification wrong.

We use Anthropic models for classification and reporting. They assure us they do not train on your data and that it will be deleted after 30 days. No data is stored.
